## 1. Circle the correct alternative

1. I **shouldn't** buy this dress as I can't really afford it.
2. You **must** have a receipt if you want to return the item.
3. We **don't have to** pay in cash — they accept credit cards.
4. I'm sorry, but the bank **isn't allowed to** give you a loan at the moment.
5. If we're going to take a taxi, you **'d better** take some cash.
6. They **ought to** check their current account to make sure they have enough money.

> *Clave:* en el 3 no hay obligación de pagar en efectivo, pero tampoco está
> prohibido; por eso `don't have to` y no `mustn't`. En el 4 la prohibición viene
> de una norma externa, de ahí `isn't allowed to`.

## 2. Complete the text with the correct form of the verbs given

Last week I had a bad banking experience. I **(1) had to pay** someone £100 in
cash, so I took the money out of a cash machine. Then the machine retained my
card. I **(2) couldn't find** a branch of the bank, so I **(3) had to call** them.
I explained that the machine **(4) shouldn't have taken** my card. They explained
that they **(5) weren't allowed to look** for the card and they ordered me a new
one. It was very annoying as I **(6) needed to get** more money out later.

> *Clave:* el texto está en pasado, así que `have to` pasa a `had to` y `can` a
> `could`. El (4) es un reproche por algo que ya ha pasado: `shouldn't have` +
> participio. El (5) es una prohibición ajena contada en pasado:
> `weren't allowed to`.

## 3. Circle the correct option

1. I think it **might be** Joe's lottery ticket because that number is his
   birthday, but I'm not sure.
2. This **can't be** Freddy's jacket. He only wears designer clothes and this one
   is definitely not designer!
3. This **must be** Jane's bag because this is her phone.
4. She's sorry, but she **might not** be able to go on the trip after all.
5. This bill **can't** be right. We didn't order five pizzas, did we?
6. This **must** be a mistake — surely this price isn't right.

> *Clave:* `must` cuando la deducción es segura, `might` cuando hay duda y
> `can't` cuando es imposible. En el (4) `must not` no encaja porque no es una
> prohibición, sino la posibilidad de que no pueda ir.

## 4. Find and correct the mistakes

1. Don't buy her that book. I think she **might have read** it already.
   *(sobraba `to`: después de un modal perfecto va el participio, no el
   infinitivo)*
2. You **must have had** a lot of money to afford that. It cost over £200,
   didn't it? *(el modal perfecto se forma con `have` + participio, no con
   `had`)*
3. They **can't have left** without paying the bill. That's a crime!
   *(`have` va seguido del participio `left`)*
4. He **could have asked** for a loan to help him pay the bills. *(la frase
   original estaba en pasiva; aquí el sujeto es quien pide el préstamo)*
5. I **may not have paid** the bill on time, which is why the bank has charged
   us. *(faltaba `have` antes del participio)*
6. You **can't have forgotten** to repay the loan. It was in the diary and on
   the calendar. *(`can't` + `have` + participio)*

## 5. Complete the text with these words

> couldn't help · could easily have got lost · had better have · had needed ·
> might be · might have forgotten · must be · must have been · should always take

**Twice a winner!**

Tim and Nina Jones now think Tuesday **(1) must be** their lucky day. They both
bought lottery tickets and used the same lucky numbers. After the Tuesday draw,
Tim checked his ticket and realised he had won £500,000. Although Nina was
asleep, Tim woke her up. She said, "You **(2) had better have** a good reason for
waking me up!" A few days later they found the second ticket in a kitchen drawer.
Nina **(3) must have been** so happy with the first win that she forgot she had
another ticket. She was lucky that she **(4) had needed** something from the
drawer and found the ticket. Otherwise, they **(5) might have forgotten** all
about it. She said the ticket **(6) could easily have got lost** when she tidied
up. She said she **(7) couldn't help** feeling that someone was looking after her.
Their motto now is that you **(8) should always take** a chance and give something
a go. You never know when you **(9) might be** a winner! As the saying goes: "You
**(10) must be** in it to win it!"

> *Clave:* `must be` se repite en (1) y (10) porque en los dos casos expresa una
> conclusión lógica en presente. El resto de palabras se usa una sola vez.
